Five rules for every one of these

The list is worth nothing without these. Rule two is the one that decides year two.

01

Compare base, never OTE

On-target earnings hide a fifth of the number behind targets you do not control. One posting advertised $237,375 at an 80/20 split. The base was $75k to $92k.

“Is that base, or on-target? What is the split, and what share of the team actually earned the variable last year?”

02

Ask the portability question first

A portable job makes year two a change of address. A job that is not portable makes year two another job hunt. That property is worth more than $15,000 of salary.

“If I moved elsewhere in the Americas in about a year, could you keep employing me, directly or through an employer of record?”

03

Lead with the pairing, not the resume

Customer-facing experience plus a practice of shipping working systems with preflight checks and failover. Few applicants have both halves.

“I build the thing, and I can explain it to the person signing the cheque, in their words.”

04

Treat a years requirement as a filter, not a fence

Four in ten of these state a number. At companies under a few hundred people it usually means “not a bootcamp grad”. Apply anyway when the content matches what you actually do.

“I have not been paid for it yet. Here is the system, running, with the receipts.”

05

Send in batches of five

One application is a referendum on you. Five in a week is a process with a response rate. Star five below, send them the same morning, then move on to the next five.

Two blockers first: one public repo with a two minute demo, and a resume aimed at the target title.

How this was built, and what it cannot tell you

  • Rebuilt by the CareerOS daemon after every daily run, from the configured sources and employer boards. Roles are selected by title family, pay floor and lane, then ranked for the year plan rather than for salary.
  • The experience wall is reported, never used to filter. Some of these will be a stretch, so the page tells you how far above the bar each one sits and leaves the decision with you.
  • Pay marked published came from the posting. Pay marked estimated was inferred from the title and the market, so treat it as a starting guess and confirm on the first call.
  • Where a posting quoted on-target earnings, the base shown strips the variable half. Two Elastic roles publish a 70/30 mix in the body text; that real split is used here rather than the parser’s 80/20 default. Grafana publishes no split, so 80/20 is assumed and flagged.
  • A posting that quotes on-target earnings without ever writing the word still reads as base. Render’s $230k to $300k Solutions Engineer is the known example, and it is not on this list.
  • Take-home figures use EXAMPLE settings: a flat 30 percent at home base and an illustrative foreign-earned-income exclusion abroad. Change the constants in the page script to your own. They are estimates, not tax advice.
  • Portability marked ask means the posting is silent, not that the answer is no. Rule two exists to turn those into a yes or a no before you invest an interview loop.
